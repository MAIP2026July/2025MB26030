# AI Collaboration Log

## Tools Used

ChatGPT was used to structure and draft assignment documents, design portal wireframes, generate and refine the local prototype code, prepare synthetic demonstration cases and support checks of the deliverables. Supporting document-generation, rendering and browser-testing utilities were used through ChatGPT. No use of Claude, Gemini, Copilot, Cursor, v0 or Replit is claimed.

The product idea, business context, intended workflow, priorities and substantive feedback were provided by me. AI helped translate my ideas into structured PM artifacts, prototype designs and a runnable local demonstrator. It supported the design and implementation process; it did not originate the product direction or make the final product decisions on my behalf.

## Where AI Helped

- **Discovery:** Organized my description of the model validation workflow into the target user, problem statement, job to be done and first use case. This was problem structuring based on my input, not independent customer research or interviews.

- **Drafting:** Converted my ideas and feedback into the executive summary, product strategy, PM Artifact Pack, UX and workflow design, data and evaluation strategy, risk note and prototype documentation. Revised the materials to follow the assignment templates.

- **Wireframing:** Translated my requirement to use the existing model inventory portal into proposed report-list and evidence-review screens. Made the background-check status, source citations, uncertainty and validator decisions visible.

- **Coding:** Generated the local browser-based demonstrator, synthetic report scenarios, lexical retrieval and scripted checks, review-decision controls, version-specific records and evidence-note export. Prepared setup instructions, a demonstration guide and an AI self-evaluation.

- **Metrics:** Formalized my focus on high true-positive gap detection, efficiency and productive utilization into measurable definitions. Suggested recall, precision, review-time and utilization targets, with false-positive and quality checks. These targets remain proposals rather than achieved results.

- **Risk review:** Helped document confidentiality, uneven detection quality, unsupported conclusions, overreliance and misuse. Converted my feedback on uncertain mappings into a High validator-review priority, an unresolved status and a requirement for human confirmation before declaring a gap.

## Example Prompts or Workflows

The examples below summarize my instructions and the iterative workflow. They are generalized descriptions, not verbatim quotations or a complete transcript.

- **Prompt 1 - Product framing and internal build:** Structure an assistant that uses validator-created guidance to cross-check developer-submitted reports and identify potential gaps. Keep the product internally built and tested because model reports, policies and governance questions are confidential.

- **Prompt 2 - Workflow and trust design:** Use the existing model inventory portal as the interface. Trigger the check automatically when a new report is submitted for a model, make the results available to the validator and give uncertain guidance mappings a higher review priority before a gap is confirmed.

- **Prompt 3 - Artifacts, prototype and refinement:** Follow the supplied assignment templates, prepare the documents and prototype, and define success through genuine-gap detection, increased efficiency and productive utilization. Revise recommendations that are too complex or do not clearly express the intended next step.

The work proceeded section by section. I supplied the direction and requirements, reviewed the framing and requested corrections; AI structured the content and produced revised artifacts and prototype materials. The local demonstrator was then checked against controlled synthetic cases and through browser interactions. These checks do not substitute for independent user research or model-quality evaluation.

## Where AI Was Weak or Wrong

- **Generic outputs:** Early framing emphasized evidence retrieval more than the intended cross-check of developer reports against validator-created guidance. My feedback made the purpose and first-pass gap-review workflow more specific.

- **Hallucinated facts and unsupported claims:** No fabricated customer interviews, bank deployment, revenue or independently validated performance should be claimed. The prototype uses synthetic cases and lexical rules, not a deployed language-model assessor. AI-generated source references and factual claims in the wider assignment still require verification before submission; this log does not claim that I independently verified every reference.

- **Weak prioritization:** Initial drafts included more detail and options than the requested templates required. The priority needed to stay on the core report-to-guidance review loop, confidentiality, traceability and human judgment. AI's priority labels and effort estimates remain proposals to assess, rather than established delivery facts.

- **Unrealistic recommendations:** An earlier executive recommendation introduced funding and evaluation-sprint language that distracted from the immediate assignment objective. I challenged it, and the recommendation was revised to developing and evaluating a focused prototype before a supervised pilot.

The code also has practical limits: lexical matching can retrieve an irrelevant passage, and scripted checks cannot reliably establish evidence sufficiency. The controlled checks were authored by the same AI that produced the implementation, so passing them is not independent proof of detection quality or security.

## Human Corrections

- **What we changed:** I clarified the core idea as checking developers' reports against validator guidance; selected an internal build; placed the interaction in the model inventory portal; specified automatic triggering on a new report submission; emphasized true positives, efficiency and utilization; and required high-priority validator checking when a mapping is uncertain. I also asked for simpler recommendations and consistent use of the assignment templates.

- **Why we changed it:** These changes aligned the materials with the actual validation workflow, the sensitivity of internal information and the need to retain human control. They also made the assignment's product logic clearer and prevented a retrieved passage or an uncertainty flag from being treated as a confirmed gap.

- **What judgment was applied:** I supplied the domain context and decided the intended user experience, deployment approach, outcomes and review responsibilities. AI translated those decisions into designs, wording and code. Proposed technical methods, numerical thresholds and implementation details still need human assessment before institutional use. No completed bank pilot, formal approval or independent validation is implied.

## Final Reflection

- **Where AI accelerated PM work:** AI helped turn my ideas into consistent documents, tables, wireframes and a runnable demonstrator, and made iterative revisions faster. It supported the translation from a business workflow to requirements, review controls, evaluation measures and prototype behavior.

- **Where AI could not replace PM reasoning:** AI could not establish the institution's actual workflow pain, approve confidential-data use, determine the significance of a real validation gap, confirm customer value or authorize deployment. My ideas and feedback provided the product direction; the accountable human team must assess assumptions, verify evidence and decide whether the product deserves further investment. AI-generated materials are inputs to that judgment, not its replacement.
